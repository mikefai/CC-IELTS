| 12 | **A** | Lindqvist | isolated birds "retained the rough species-typical rhythm but lacked its detailed structure"; deafened birds "bore almost no resemblance to normal song" |

**Repeated:** A (Lindqvist) ×2 · D (Takahashi) ×2
**Unused:** E (Ferreira)

### ⚠️ The designed trap — Question 11

Q11 concerns cities. Ferreira is the city researcher. The paragraph is Ferreira's paragraph.
Almost every weaker candidate answers **E**.

The answer is **D**, because the passage says *"**Takahashi** has suggested that some urban
populations may now be forming dialects of their own"* — a second researcher quoted inside
the first researcher's paragraph.

**Teach this as a rule:** matching-to-researcher tests *who made the claim*, never *whose
paragraph it sits in*. Read to the attribution verb before committing.

### ⚠️ The overlap traps

| Confusable pair | Why they overlap | What separates them |
|---|---|---|
| Lindqvist / Corrigan | Both discuss what exists before learning | Lindqvist = a template **acquired** by listening. Corrigan = a bias **inherited** at hatching. |
| Osei / Ferreira | Both show environment shaping song | Osei = the **social** environment (a live tutor). Ferreira = the **acoustic** environment (traffic noise). |
| Takahashi / Ferreira | Both concern geographic variation | Takahashi = **dialects as tradition**. Ferreira = **immediate physical adjustment**. |

Q12 is answerable only if the candidate notices that two *different* deprivations
(isolation, deafening) produced two *different* severities. A candidate who reads only the
first half of the paragraph will still choose A, but for the wrong reason — worth probing
in feedback.

## Questions 13–15 · Multiple-answer matching

**Answers: A, C, D** — in any order. One mark each.

All three come from a single sentence in paragraph **G**:

> "birds in noisy districts sing at a **higher pitch**, in **shorter phrases**, and more
> often at **dawn before traffic builds**"

| Distractor | Why it is wrong |
|---|---|
| **B** — increase in loudness | The passage says low notes are *masked*. It never says the birds sing louder. Logical inference ≠ stated information. |
| **E** — fewer singing birds | Nothing in the passage addresses numbers of birds. |
| **F** — imitating mechanical sounds | True of some real species, absent from this passage. Punishes outside knowledge. |
| **G** — longer gaps between songs | The passage says shorter *phrases*, not longer *gaps*. Same semantic field, different claim. |

## Strategy guide

### Matching information to paragraphs

1. **Do this task last.** It requires whole-passage scanning; by answering the other tasks
   first the candidate already knows the territory. Working in printed order typically
   costs 4–5 minutes.
2. **Underline the abstract noun**, not the topic: *a comparison* (Q1), *an admission* (Q5),
   *a description* (Q6). That word says what kind of sentence to hunt for.
3. **Letters may repeat; paragraphs may go unused.** Read the rubric every single time — it
   changes between tests.

### Matching statements to researchers

1. **Circle every proper name before reading the questions.** Thirty seconds converts the
   task from reading into targeted checking.
2. **Track names that appear outside their own paragraph.** This is the primary difficulty
   mechanism in this task type, and it produces Q11.
3. **Follow the attribution verb** — *argues, concluded, indicates, has suggested, is careful
   to note*. The name attached to the verb owns the claim.
4. **Expect overlap.** If a statement sounds like two researchers, the distinguishing detail
   has not been found yet. Do not guess from topic.
5. **An unused option is normal.** Do not redistribute answers to use every name.

### Multiple-answer matching

1. **Answers cluster.** Find one, then finish the sentence before searching elsewhere.
2. **Reject inferences.** Only literal presence counts.
3. **Watch near-miss vocabulary** (*shorter phrases* vs. *longer gaps*).
4. **Never leave one blank** — each letter is marked independently and there is no penalty.

### Suggested 20-minute route

| Min | Action |
|---|---|
| 0–2 | Skim A–H. Two-word summary beside each paragraph. Circle every name. |
| 2–9 | Q7–12 (names already located). |
| 9–13 | Q13–15 (locate the urban-noise paragraph, read closely). |
| 13–19 | Q1–6, using the summaries. |
| 19–20 | Transfer and check. No box left empty. |

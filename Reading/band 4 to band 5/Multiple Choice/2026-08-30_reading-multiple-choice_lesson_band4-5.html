IELTS Reading: Multiple Choice

Finding the Correct Answer Among Options

Target: Band 4.0 – 5.5
Band 4-5 Focus: Identify main ideas and specific details. Understand synonyms and paraphrasing. Read carefully to avoid false answers.

Understanding Multiple Choice

Format: Read a passage. Answer questions by choosing from A, B, C, or D.

Challenge: Multiple answers may seem correct—you must find the BEST answer based on the text.

Key Skills: Scanning for information, understanding paraphrasing, avoiding distractors.

Why Students Lose Marks

  • Choosing answers that sound true but aren't in the text
  • Misreading question words (not, best, main)
  • Selecting partially correct answers when another is more complete
  • Not reading all options before deciding

Band 4-5 Strategies

Strategy 1: Find Keywords in the Question ▼
What to do: Underline the main noun and verb in the question. Then scan the passage for the same words (or synonyms).

Example:
Question: "Why did scientists study the migration?"
Keywords: "scientists," "why," "migration"
Scan the text for these words or similar meanings like "researchers," "reason," "movement."
Strategy 2: Eliminate Wrong Answers ▼
What to do: Cross out answers that are:
• Not mentioned in the text
• Opposite to the passage
• True but not relevant to the question

This leaves 1-2 options to choose between carefully.
Strategy 3: Understand Paraphrasing ▼
What to do: The correct answer often uses different words but the same meaning as the passage.

Text: "The company expanded rapidly between 2010 and 2015."
Question: "What happened to the company during this period?"
Correct answer: "It grew quickly" (not the exact words, but same meaning)

Practice Questions

Passage:

Coffee was first introduced to Europe in the 17th century through trade routes from the Middle East. Unlike tea, which was already familiar, coffee was entirely new to Europeans. At first, only wealthy merchants could afford it. However, by the 1700s, coffeehouses became popular meeting places where people discussed business, politics, and ideas. This social aspect made coffee drinking more than just a beverage—it became a symbol of social status and intellectual culture.

Q1: Why was coffee initially available only to wealthy people? ▼
A) Because coffeehouses refused to serve poor customers
B) Because it was expensive and imported from distant places
C) Because the government restricted its sale
D) Because coffee was not popular in Europe yet

Answer: B
Why: The passage says "only wealthy merchants could afford it," implying it was expensive. The text mentions trade routes (import) and limited availability support this.
Why not A: The text doesn't mention coffeehouses restricting service; it only says wealthy people could afford it (price issue).
Q2: What does "symbol of social status" mean in this context? ▼
A) Coffee was only consumed by the government
B) Drinking coffee showed a person's wealth and importance
C) Coffee was used only in religious ceremonies
D) The symbol of coffee was displayed on all European flags

Answer: B
Why: "Social status" refers to a person's rank or position in society. Drinking coffee showed wealth (it was expensive) and sophistication (discussed in intellectual coffeehouses).
Band 4-5 tip: Look for synonyms: "status" = rank, position, importance.
Q3: The passage suggests that coffeehouses were important for which reason? ▼
A) They were cheaper than buying coffee at home
B) They served tea and coffee equally
C) They became places where important discussions and ideas were shared
D) They were only open to wealthy merchants

Answer: C
Why: The text says coffeehouses were "meeting places where people discussed business, politics, and ideas." This shows their social and intellectual importance.
Reading skill: Paraphrase—the passage uses "meeting places" and "discussed," while the answer uses "where important discussions."

Practice Tips

Before you answer:
  • Read the question first (before the passage if time allows)
  • Underline keywords
  • Find the relevant part of the passage
  • Read all four options
  • Choose the BEST answer, not just a correct-sounding one
Time management:
  • Spend 20 minutes on 1 passage (3-4 questions)
  • Don't re-read the entire passage for every question
  • Use scanning (quickly find the key sentence) not reading every word

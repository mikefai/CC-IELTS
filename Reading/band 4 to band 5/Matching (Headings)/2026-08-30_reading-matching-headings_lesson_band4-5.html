IELTS Reading: Matching Headings

Finding the Main Idea of Each Paragraph

Target: Band 4.0 – 5.5
Band 4-5 Focus: Understand main ideas, not details. Match the overall topic of each paragraph. Learn to ignore minor information.

Understanding Matching Headings

Format: You receive 7-8 headings. Each paragraph needs one heading. Some headings are NOT used.

Challenge: Headings are often general (main idea) while paragraphs contain specific details. You must ignore details.

Key Skills: Finding main ideas, recognizing paraphrasing, avoiding false matches.

Why Students Lose Marks

  • Matching based on ONE detail instead of the main idea
  • Not reading all headings before deciding
  • Choosing headings that are partly correct but too narrow
  • Confusing similar headings (e.g., "Benefits of X" vs. "Dangers of X")

Band 4-5 Strategies

Strategy 1: Read Only the First and Last Sentence ▼
What to do: The main idea is usually in the first sentence (topic sentence) or last sentence (conclusion). Skip the middle details at first.

Example:
First sentence: "Climate change threatens coral reefs worldwide."
→ Main idea is THREATS or DANGERS, not specific data about temperature.
Strategy 2: Ask "What is This Paragraph About?" Not "What Details Are Here?" ▼
What to do: After reading, think of the BIG picture, not specific facts.

Paragraph details: "Scientists used X method. They measured Y. Results showed Z percentage."
Big picture (main idea): "Research Methods" or "Finding Solutions"
This is what the heading should match.
Strategy 3: Use Keyword Matching Carefully ▼
What to do: If a heading uses the same key word as the paragraph, that's a CLUE, not proof. The main idea must match, too.

Heading: "The Benefits of Urban Gardens"
Paragraph: "Urban gardens require little water..." (talks about requirements, not benefits)
✗ Wrong match (same topic, different idea)

Practice Example

Headings:

A) How bees communicate with other bees

B) The importance of bees to human food production

C) Different species of bees around the world

D) What plants bees visit most often

E) The threats facing bee populations today

Paragraph 1:

One-third of the food we eat depends on pollination by bees. From almonds to cucumbers, apples to cherries, many crops cannot produce fruit without bees visiting their flowers. Without bees, supermarket shelves would look very different, and food prices would rise dramatically. This is why protecting bee populations is a global priority.
Which heading matches this paragraph? ▼
Answer: B) The importance of bees to human food production

Why: The paragraph talks about how much food depends on bees. It lists crops (almonds, apples, etc.) as examples, but the MAIN idea is that bees are essential for food.

Why not A: The paragraph doesn't explain HOW bees communicate.
Why not D: Specific plants are mentioned, but that's not the main point.

Paragraph 2:

Over the past decade, bee populations have declined by 25% in many regions. Habitat loss, pesticides, and climate change are the primary causes. Scientists warn that if this trend continues, the ecological consequences could be severe. Several conservation programs are now active in Europe and North America to reverse this decline.
Which heading matches this paragraph? ▼
Answer: E) The threats facing bee populations today

Why: The main idea is PROBLEMS (pesticides, habitat loss) and DECLINE. The paragraph explains what is threatening bees.

Important: The paragraph mentions conservation programs, but that's not the main focus—it's just additional context. The focus is on the THREATS.

Practice Tips

Step-by-step approach:
  • Read the heading list FIRST to understand the topics available
  • Read paragraph 1 (first and last sentence only initially)
  • Ask: "What is the MAIN topic?" in one phrase
  • Match to the heading that fits that main topic
  • Cross out the heading (you can only use each once)
  • Move to the next paragraph
Red flags (often WRONG matches):
  • The heading uses the same word but wrong context
  • The heading matches only ONE detail, not the overall idea
  • The heading is too narrow (focuses on minor information)
Time management:
  • Spend 15 minutes on this task (5-7 paragraphs)
  • Read headings first (1 minute)
  • Match each paragraph (2 minutes per paragraph)

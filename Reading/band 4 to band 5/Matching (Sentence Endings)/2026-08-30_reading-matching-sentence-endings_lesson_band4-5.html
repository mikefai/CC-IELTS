IELTS Reading: Matching Sentence Endings

Completing Sentence Fragments with the Right Ending

Target: Band 4.0 – 5.5
Band 4-5 Focus: Find where information appears. Match sentence structure and meaning. Read for specific details.

Understanding Matching Sentence Endings

Format: You receive sentence beginnings and endings. Match each beginning to the correct ending from the options. Some endings are NOT used.

Challenge: Similar endings can mislead you. You must match based on exact meaning in the passage, not general logic.

Key Skills: Finding specific information, understanding grammar connections, recognizing synonyms.

Why Students Lose Marks

  • Choosing endings that make sense in general English, but don't match the passage
  • Matching based on grammar (the sentence "sounds right") instead of passage meaning
  • Not finding where the information appears in the text
  • Using the same ending twice by mistake

Band 4-5 Strategies

Strategy 1: Find the Sentence Beginning in the Passage ▼
What to do: Scan the passage to find each sentence beginning (or a very similar version). The ending you need comes RIGHT AFTER that location in the passage.

Example:
Sentence beginning: "The company's success..."
You find in passage: "The company's success was due to..."
The ending should match what comes next: "...investment in technology"
Strategy 2: Use Keywords from the Sentence Beginning ▼
What to do: Underline the KEY noun or verb in the sentence beginning. Search for that word (or a synonym) in the passage.

Sentence beginning: "Research into climate change..."
Keywords: "Research," "climate change"
Search for these words in the passage to find where this idea is discussed.
Strategy 3: Match Grammar AND Meaning ▼
What to do: The ending must: (1) Complete the sentence grammatically, AND (2) Match the passage meaning.

Sentence: "The ancient city was built on..."
Option A: "...sandy soil" (grammatically correct, but passage says "rock")
Option B: "...rock formations" (matches the passage)
✓ Choose B, not A.

Practice Example

Passage:

The invention of the printing press in 1440 revolutionized the spread of information. Before this innovation, books were copied by hand, a process that took months. Only wealthy people could afford such expensive texts. The printing press changed this dramatically by allowing many copies to be produced quickly and cheaply. As a result, books became accessible to the general population, and literacy rates began to rise significantly across Europe. This development contributed directly to the success of the Renaissance and the scientific revolution.

Available Endings:

A) ...were owned only by wealthy people

B) ...took several months to complete

C) ...led to increased education levels

D) ...were easier to read than printed books

Sentence 1: Before the printing press, books... ▼
Answer: B) ...took several months to complete

Why: The passage states "books were copied by hand, a process that took months." This directly matches the answer.

Why not A: While the passage says only wealthy could afford books, the sentence is about HOW books were made, not who owned them.
Sentence 2: The printing press made books widely available, which... ▼
Answer: C) ...led to increased education levels

Why: The passage says "literacy rates began to rise significantly" and books became "accessible to the general population." This matches the meaning of increased education levels (literacy = education).

Important: The passage uses "literacy rates" but the answer uses "education levels." These are synonyms in this context—one common test strategy.

Practice Tips

Step-by-step approach:
  • Read sentence beginning 1
  • Identify key words (noun/verb)
  • Scan passage to find where this is discussed
  • Read what comes next in the passage
  • Match to an ending that captures this information
  • Check: Does the grammar work? Does the meaning match?
  • Cross out the ending (each can only be used once)
Common mistake:
  • Choosing an ending that "sounds correct" but comes from general knowledge, not the passage
  • Solution: Always point to the exact sentence in the passage that supports your answer
Time management:
  • Spend 10 minutes on this task (5-6 sentence pairs)
  • Quickly scan for each sentence beginning (1 minute total)
  • Match each ending (1-2 minutes per pair)

How this question type works

What you have to do

You read a short summary of part of the passage. Some words are missing. You fill each gap so the summary stays true to the passage and is grammatically correct. There are two versions:

Task 1 — from a list. Choose your answers from the box of words. There are more words than gaps, so some words are not used. Write the word, not the letter.

Task 2 — from the passage. Use words taken directly from the passage. Write NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS for each answer. Do not change the form of the word.
Working method Read the whole summary first. Decide what kind of word each gap needs — a noun, a verb, an adjective? Then find the part of the passage the summary is describing and match the meaning, not just the same words.
Reading passage

The Return of the City Bee

AOver the past twenty years, keeping bees in cities has become surprisingly popular. Rooftops, balconies and community gardens in London, Paris and New York now hold thousands of hives. For many people, the main attraction is the chance to produce their own honey. Others keep bees because they are worried about the decline of pollinating insects and want to help.

BCity bees often do better than country bees. This seems strange, but the reason is simple. Farmland is frequently planted with a single crop, so once that crop has finished flowering there is little food left. Cities, by contrast, contain parks, gardens and street trees that flower at different times from spring to autumn. This variety gives bees nectar for most of the year.

CTemperature also plays a part. Buildings, roads and pavements store heat during the day and release it slowly at night, making urban areas a few degrees warmer than the surrounding countryside. This effect, known as the urban heat island, allows city bees to start flying earlier in the year and to stay active later in the day.

DThere are problems too. If too many hives are placed in a small area, the bees compete with each other and with wild pollinators for the same flowers. Some experts now argue that planting more flowers matters more than adding more hives. A single hive can contain up to 50,000 bees, and each colony needs a large area of blossom to survive.

EPollution is a further concern. Bees that feed near busy roads may collect tiny amounts of metal from vehicle exhaust, and traces have been found in some urban honey. The levels are usually far below the point at which they would harm a person, but researchers continue to monitor them.

FDespite these difficulties, most beekeepers believe the benefits are worth the effort. Bees pollinate the fruit trees and vegetable plots that make city green spaces productive, and looking after a hive encourages people to pay attention to the seasons and the weather. For city dwellers with little contact with nature, that connection may be the greatest reward of all.

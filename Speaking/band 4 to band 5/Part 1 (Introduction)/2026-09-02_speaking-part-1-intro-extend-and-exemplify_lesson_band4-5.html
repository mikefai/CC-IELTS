For the teacher — staging, timing, anticipated problems

Timing 50–60 min: Lead-in 6′ (activate topic & predict) → Input 10′ (3 strategies modelled) → Controlled 14′ (timed drill Q1–4) → Feedback 10′ (reveal + why distractors fail) → Production/retake 10–15′.

Staging 3 blocks: (1) Noticing — underline question words / paraphrase targets. (2) Doing — timed drill with strategy checklist. (3) Reflecting — reveal, self-score, retake one question with corrected method.

Anticipated problems ×3: (1) Word-matching trap → fix: train “same meaning, different words” with synonym list. (2) Waiting for exact words → fix: predict synonyms before listening/reading. (3) Over-long answers or inference beyond text → fix: enforce word limit / “literal presence only”.

Student HTML carries no answers; key is separate. Reveal is student-controlled after attempt. Timer is practice pacing, not exam pressure.

Strategy — 3 techniques (only 3)

1
Show range, not just vocab
Mix tenses (present perfect, conditionals), complex sentences (although/whereas/which), and precise vocabulary.
2
Concede and lean
In Part 3, acknowledge the other side briefly, then state your lean: 'While X has merit, I largely think Y because...'
3
Extend with example
Answer → explain why → give a concrete example → add a result or feeling. One sentence is never enough at Band 6+.

Guided example (worked)

Worked — how to apply the 3
Step 1 — Predict: Read Q1 stem, underline what is asked (time? place? number?). Say one possible answer.
Step 2 — Paraphrase hunt: The correct option uses synonyms — “commences at half past five” = 5:30.
Step 3 — Trap check: The distractor is mentioned then corrected (“we planned six — now eight”) — pick the second value.

Practice — timed drill

6:00 Do Q1–4 under time, then Check.

Task — Part 1 at Band 4–5: record yourself 3 × 1-minute answers. Use the strategy checklist after each take.

Prompt 1 (familiar): Describe a place you enjoy visiting. — 1 min, extend with example + feeling.
Prompt 2 (less familiar): Talk about a decision you made recently. — 1 min, explain why + result.
Prompt 3 (Part 3 style): Why do some people prefer routine over variety? — 1 min, give both sides then lean.

6:00
Model language (click)

Extend: “I really enjoy going to the old market in my city because it feels alive — for example, last month I spent an afternoon there and ended up trying food I'd never seen before, which made the visit memorable.”

Fill: “Well, actually, I suppose the main reason I chose that was …” rather than silence.

Lean in Part 3: “While routine clearly suits some people because it feels safe, I largely think variety matters more nowadays since jobs and technology change so quickly.”

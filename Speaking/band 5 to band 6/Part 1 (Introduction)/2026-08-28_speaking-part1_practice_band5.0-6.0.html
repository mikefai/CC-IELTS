Part 1 — Everyday Topics

Five familiar topics, twenty questions. Practise answering out loud.

ExamIELTS Academic
SectionSpeaking, Part 1
Format3 topics in the real test
Target band5.0 – 6.0
Part 1 length4 – 5 minutes
Questions here20
How Part 1 works

What to expect

Part 1 comes first. The examiner asks you short questions about familiar topics — your life, your habits, your likes and dislikes. There is no preparation time; you answer straight away.

Give two or three sentences per answer. One sentence is too short; a long speech is not needed here. A good shape is: answer + reason + example or extra detail.
Practice method Record yourself answering each question. Play it back and check: did you answer for about 15–20 seconds? Did you give a reason? Then open the sample answer and compare.
Topic 1

Your hometown

1 Hometown

  1. Where is your hometown?
  2. What do you like most about it?
  3. Is it a good place for young people to live? Why?
  4. Would you like to live there in the future?
Show a band 5.5–6 sample answer

Where is your hometown?My hometown is Bursa. It's a city in the north-west of Turkey, about two hours from Istanbul by car. It's quite large, but the area where I grew up is calm and green.

What do you like most about it?I think the best thing is the food. Bursa is famous for a dish called İskender kebab, and there are a lot of small family restaurants. I also like that the mountains are close, so you can go skiing in winter.

Is it a good place for young people to live?It's good in some ways. There's a university and some nice cafés, so students have things to do. But there aren't many big companies, so a lot of young people move to Istanbul to find better jobs.

Would you like to live there in the future?Maybe when I'm older. Right now I want to work in a bigger city, but Bursa is a nice place to have a family because it's cheaper and less crowded.

Phrases to reuse: It's famous for…in some ways… but…a lot of people move to… to find…
Topic 2

Work or studies

2 Work or studies

  1. Do you work or are you a student?
  2. Why did you choose this subject / job?
  3. What is the most difficult part of it?
  4. What would you like to do after you finish?
Show a band 5.5–6 sample answer

Do you work or are you a student?I'm a student. I'm in my third year at university, and I'm studying business administration.

Why did you choose this subject?I chose it because it's quite general, so it keeps my options open. My father has a small import business, and I've always been interested in how companies are managed.

What is the most difficult part of it?For me the hardest part is the statistics and accounting modules. I'm not very confident with numbers, so I have to study those subjects much longer than the others.

What would you like to do after you finish?I'd like to work in marketing for an international company. First I want to get some experience here, and then maybe do a master's degree abroad.

Phrases to reuse: it keeps my options openI've always been interested in…I have to study… much longer than…
Topic 3

Free time

3 Free time

  1. What do you usually do in your free time?
  2. Do you prefer to spend free time alone or with other people?
  3. Has the way you spend your free time changed since you were a child?
  4. Do you think people have enough free time these days?
Show a band 5.5–6 sample answer

What do you usually do in your free time?It depends on the day. During the week I usually just watch series or play football with my friends. At the weekend I try to go outside more, for example hiking or visiting my grandparents.

Do you prefer to spend free time alone or with other people?Mostly with other people. I enjoy being with my friends because we laugh a lot. But sometimes after a busy week I need a quiet evening alone to relax.

Has the way you spend your free time changed since you were a child?Yes, quite a lot. When I was a child I played outside all day with the kids in my street. Now I spend more time on my phone, which is not really a good change.

Do you think people have enough free time these days?I don't think so. Most people I know work long hours or study a lot, so by the evening they're too tired to do anything. Weekends go very quickly.

Phrases to reuse: It depends on the daywhich is not really a good changeby the evening they're too tired to…
Topic 4

Food

4 Food

  1. What kind of food do you like?
  2. Do you prefer eating at home or in a restaurant?
  3. Can you cook? Who taught you?
  4. Has the food people eat in your country changed in recent years?
Show a band 5.5–6 sample answer

What kind of food do you like?I like most food, but my favourite is probably traditional Turkish food, like grilled meat and vegetable dishes with olive oil. I don't really enjoy very spicy food.

Do you prefer eating at home or in a restaurant?At home, usually. The food is healthier and cheaper, and I like eating with my family. I go to restaurants mainly for special occasions or to try something different.

Can you cook? Who taught you?I can cook simple things — pasta, eggs, some rice dishes. My mother taught me the basics before I moved out for university, because she didn't want me to eat fast food every day.

Has the food people eat in your country changed in recent years?Yes, I think so. There are many more foreign restaurants now, like sushi and burgers, especially in big cities. Young people also order food online much more than before.

Phrases to reuse: my favourite is probably…mainly for special occasionsmuch more than before
Topic 5

Technology

5 Technology

  1. How often do you use your phone during the day?
  2. What do you mainly use the internet for?
  3. Is there any technology you would like to learn to use better?
  4. Do you think people rely on technology too much?
Show a band 5.5–6 sample answer

How often do you use your phone during the day?Honestly, too often. I check it as soon as I wake up, and then many times during the day for messages and social media. I think my screen time is around five hours.

What do you mainly use the internet for?Mostly for studying and communication. I watch video lessons for my university course, and I use messaging apps to talk to my family and friends. I also read the news every morning.

Is there any technology you would like to learn to use better?Yes, I'd like to be better at Excel. We use it in some of my classes, and I know it can do a lot of useful things, but I only know the basic functions.

Do you think people rely on technology too much?In some ways, yes. For example, I can't remember any phone numbers now, and I feel lost without maps on my phone. But technology also helps a lot, so it's difficult to say it's completely bad.

Phrases to reuse: Honestly, too oftenI only know the basic functionsit's difficult to say it's completely bad

Multiple Choice: Beat the Distractors

A listening-first lesson for band 5→6. Predict answers, catch paraphrases, and eliminate traps. 3 strategies only — master these before adding more.

Today's three strategies

1
Predict before you listen

Read the question and underline the thing being asked (a time? an object? a place?). Say two possible answers in your head. Predicted ears catch paraphrase; unprepared ears catch nothing.

2
Listen for the paraphrase, not the words

The correct answer almost never uses the speaker's exact words. "starts at half past" → 5:30. Match meaning, not sound.

3
Expect the trap, then the correction

Speakers mention a wrong option, then correct themselves: "we planned six — no, it's eight now". The second mention is usually the answer.

Practice: campus library announcement

Scenario: an announcement about a study-skills workshop. Predict an answer for each question before you open the transcript.

1. What time does the workshop start?
2. What must students bring with them?
3. Where will the workshop be held?
4. How many places are still available?
Transcript (read aloud, or have a partner read it to you)
Good afternoon everyone. A quick reminder that the library's study-skills workshop — the one on taking better lecture notes — begins at half past five this evening. We had planned an earlier start at five, but the tutor is teaching until five fifteen, so we've pushed it back slightly. Please remember to bring your library card, as you'll need it to borrow the course pack afterwards. Laptops are welcome but not essential, and notebooks will be provided, so there's no need to bring your own. We'll be in the main hall this time, not room 204 as in previous terms — the projector there is being replaced. And a small correction on numbers: we originally had twenty places with only six remaining, but eight more have just opened up, so there are now quite a few seats left. See you this evening.

Answer key balance check: 4 questions — one A, one B, one C, one D. No option skew. ✅

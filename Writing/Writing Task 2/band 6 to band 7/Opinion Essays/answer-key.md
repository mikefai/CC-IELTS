## Band 7 model answer (278 words)

Transport policy forces governments into a difficult choice between funding public
networks and expanding the road system. While improved roads clearly benefit some
groups, I believe that investment in public transport delivers greater long-term value.

Those who favour road building argue that it responds to how people actually travel.
In most countries the private car remains the dominant form of transport, particularly
outside major cities, and congested or poorly maintained roads impose real costs on
both commuters and businesses. Freight in particular depends almost entirely on the
road network, so upgrading it can lower the price of goods. Rural communities, which
are rarely served by rail or metro systems, also stand to gain far more from a new
road than from a bus route that may never reach them.

However, the case for public transport becomes stronger once the wider consequences
are considered. Building additional roads tends to encourage more driving, a
phenomenon known as induced demand, so congestion frequently returns within a few
years. A well-funded metro or bus network, by contrast, moves a far greater number of
passengers per unit of space and produces significantly lower emissions per journey.
It also serves those on low incomes, students and elderly people, who may not be able
to afford a car at all, and therefore benefits a broader section of society than road
spending does.

In my view, governments should prioritise public transport while maintaining existing
roads to a reasonable standard. This approach tackles congestion and pollution
simultaneously and distributes the benefits of public spending more fairly.

In conclusion, although road investment meets a genuine and immediate need, funding
public transport offers more sustainable and more equitable returns.

## Why this is Band 7, criterion by criterion

### Task Response — 7
Both views are addressed and developed with reasons *and* consequences, not just
assertions. The position appears in the introduction and never wavers. The opinion
paragraph refines the position rather than repeating it.

> *What would lift it to 8:* more specific extension — a named country, a figure, or a
> concrete case rather than "in most countries".

### Coherence and Cohesion — 7
One clear central idea per paragraph. Cohesion is carried by referencing
(`it`, `this approach`, `those who favour`) rather than by bolted-on connectors.

> *What would lift it to 8:* less predictable paragraph openings. `However,` and
> `In conclusion,` are functional but formulaic.

### Lexical Resource — 7
Some less common and precise items: `induced demand`, `freight`, `equitable`,
`per unit of space`, `stand to gain`. Collocation is accurate throughout.

> *What would lift it to 8:* one or two of these are topic-specific rather than
> flexible; Band 8 needs skilful use of uncommon lexis across a wider range.

### Grammatical Range and Accuracy — 7
A good spread: non-defining relative clause (`Rural communities, which are rarely
served...`), concessive subordination (`While improved roads...`, `although road
investment...`), and passive-plus-participle (`once the wider consequences are
considered`). Errors are rare.

> *What would lift it to 8:* the majority of sentences would need to be error-free,
> with more variation in sentence *length* as well as structure.

**Overall: Band 7.0**

## The three errors that most often hold Turkish L1 learners at Band 6

Address these in feedback before anything else — they cost more marks than vocabulary does.

**1. Missing or wrong articles.** Turkish has no definite article, so `the` is either
omitted or over-applied.
- ✗ `Government should invest in public transport.`
- ✓ `The government should invest in public transport.` / `Governments should invest...`
- ✗ `The freight depends on the road network.` (uncountable, general → no article)
- ✓ `Freight depends on the road network.`

**2. Present perfect used for finished past time.** Turkish `-mış` maps onto both.
- ✗ `In 2019 the city has built a new metro line.`
- ✓ `In 2019 the city built a new metro line.`
- ✓ `The city has built three metro lines since 2010.` (unfinished period → correct)

**3. `Which` as an all-purpose connector between sentences.**
- ✗ `Roads are expensive, which this is a problem for governments.`
- ✓ `Roads are expensive, which is a problem for governments.`
- ✓ `Roads are expensive. This is a problem for governments.`

## Marking shortcut

Give a band per criterion, then average and round to the nearest half band.
Quote **one line of the student's own writing** as evidence for each band you award —
an unevidenced band is not usable feedback.

Limit yourself to **three prioritised actions**. More than three is not remembered.

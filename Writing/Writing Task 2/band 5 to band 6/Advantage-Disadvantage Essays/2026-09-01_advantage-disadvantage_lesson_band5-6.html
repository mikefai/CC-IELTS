IELTS Academic · Writing Task 2 — Advantage–Disadvantage

Do the Advantages Outweigh the Disadvantages?

Balanced first, weighted second — then a clear thesis. One evaluated idea per paragraph.

Target band5.0 – 6.0
Time40 min · ≥250 words
Structure4 paragraphs · outweigh = judgment
Cue wordsadvantages / benefits vs disadvantages / drawbacks
For the teacher — 50–55 min staging

Aim: Students stop listing 4–5 shallow pros/cons and instead develop one weighted advantage and one weighted disadvantage, with a thesis that answers “outweigh?”.

Stages: 5′ lead-in (quick poll: “More people shop online — good or bad?”) → 7′ input (3 strategies, especially strategy 2 “evaluate, don’t list”) → 10′ Exercise A (sort 6 statements into Advantage / Disadvantage, then re-label as Major vs Minor) → 10′ Exercise B (outweigh ordering) → 15′ walkthrough (online-shopping model, weak vs strong thesis) → 10′ production (tourism prompt) → peer check vs checklist.

Anticipated: Students write “There are many advantages and disadvantages” with no evaluation — band 5. Fix: force a sentence “Overall, the benefits outweigh the drawbacks because … outweighs …” Students repeat question words — teach paraphrase bank (advantage = benefit, upside, merit; disadvantage = drawback, downside, cost).

Notes — what “outweigh” really means

Two different tasks look similar but need different theses.

WordingWhat you must doThesis shape
Discuss advantages & disadvantagesDescribe both sides; no overall judgment needed“This essay will discuss both benefits and drawbacks.”
Do advantages outweigh disadvantages?Describe both sides and give a verdict“Although there are clear drawbacks, the benefits outweigh them because …”
Do benefits outweigh costs?Same as outweighSame — weigh and decide.

Rule for this lesson: If the question contains outweigh, your introduction and conclusion must state a clear verdict.

Band 5 trap
Writing “There are advantages and disadvantages” without saying which side is stronger does not answer outweigh. That is Task Response 5, even with good grammar.

Instructions — 40-minute method for outweigh

1
5 min — Map and weigh
List 2 real advantages and 2 real disadvantages → star (*) the heaviest on each side → decide: which starred item is heavier overall? Write a one-line verdict: “Advantages outweigh because [starred advantage] is long-term and affects everyone, while [starred disadvantage] is short-term/manageable.”
2
30 min — 4 paragraphs
Intro: paraphrase → sentence 2: thesis with verdict + reason. Body 1: weaker side (one idea, fully explained + example, but note its limit). Body 2: stronger side (one idea, explained + example + evaluation: “This matters more because …”). Conclusion: restate verdict.
3
5 min — Check
Verdict in intro & conclusion matches? Each body is one weighted idea (not a list of 4)? Examples with place/number? Word count 260–280?
Paragraph shape (outweigh)
Body (weaker side): Point → Explain → Example → Concession (“This drawback is real, but limited because …”).
Body (stronger side): Point → Explain → Example → Evaluation (“This advantage outweighs because it is long-term / affects more people / cost is one-off”).

Guidance — 3 strategies (only 3)

1
Verdict first — write your thesis before body paragraphs

Decide during planning: “Overall, advantages outweigh because ___.” Use the same because to build Body 2. Without a verdict, Cohesion and Task Response stay at 5.

2
One weighted idea beats four listed points

Develop one advantage and one disadvantage fully. Evaluation language is the outweigh: more significant, longer lasting, affects the majority, by contrast this cost is temporary/manageable/avoidable.

3
Balance, then tilt

Give the weaker side fairly (don’t straw-man it), then tilt in the stronger paragraph. Examiners trust a balanced writer who still decides.

Exercise B — Build an outweigh paragraph (ordering)

Put this Body 2 (the stronger side — advantages) into Point → Explain → Example → Evaluation (outweigh) order. Click sentences in order.

Why evaluation last?
Evaluation is your outweigh. Only after you’ve proved the advantage (example) can you claim it is “more significant / longer lasting / affects more people” than the disadvantage in Body 1. Without it, the essay lists but never weighs.

Step 3 — Model body paragraph (advantage, 146 words)▸

On the positive side, the main advantage is that online retail lowers prices and greatly increases convenience for ordinary households. Because sellers compete nationally rather than on a single street and avoid high rents and staffing costs, they can pass savings to buyers while offering next-day delivery and easy price comparison. For example, families in rural Wales who previously drove 40 minutes to the nearest city can now order school supplies or medicines at midnight and receive them the following day at a lower cost. This benefit outweighs the drawback of high-street pressure because lower costs affect almost every household every week, whereas the loss of a few shops, though visible locally, does not broadly raise the cost of living and can be eased if centres adapt with services and collection points.

146 words · Point → explain → example → evaluation (outweigh). Body 1 would cover the disadvantage concessively.

Show full model answer (272 words, Band 6)
In many countries, the rise of online shopping has transformed how people buy everyday goods and whether physical shops can survive. Although the shift brings clear disadvantages for small retailers, I believe the advantages of lower prices, wider choice and daily convenience outweigh those drawbacks. Admittedly, the growth of e-commerce can damage traditional high streets. When customers move online, independent shops lose footfall and may close, which reduces local services and community life. In the United Kingdom, for instance, about fifteen percent of high-street shops closed between 2015 and 2023 as online sales expanded, and some towns now have vacant units along the main road. This drawback is real; however, it is geographically concentrated and can be mitigated. Many centres have adapted by converting units to cafés, clinics or collection hubs, and affected shop staff often move to warehouse and delivery roles that the same online demand creates. By contrast, the benefits are system-wide and daily. Because online sellers compete nationally and avoid high-street rents, they offer lower prices and allow buyers to compare alternatives within minutes, with next-day delivery to the door. For residents of rural areas — for example, families in mid-Wales who previously drove forty minutes to the nearest city — this means access at midnight to medicines or school materials at a lower cost. The time saved alone returns hours each week for work and study, while the price difference directly reduces household spending. Such gains outweigh the local cost to high streets because they affect far more people, far more often, and over the long term. In conclusion, while online shopping undeniably pressures small shops, the advantages of cheaper, more convenient and more accessible retail are broader and longer lasting. On balance, the benefits outweigh the disadvantages, particularly when high streets are supported to adapt. 272 words · Intro verdict + 2 bodies (disadvantage conceding / advantage evaluating) + conclusion verdict.

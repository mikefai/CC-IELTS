IELTS Academic Writing · Task 1 · Lesson 1

Numbers Into Words

Task 1 is not a maths test and it is not an opinion essay. It is a translation job: turn a table or a graph into 150 accurate words, in twenty minutes. This lesson teaches the one structure that does that job every time — first on a table, then on a line graph — because it is the same skill both times.

Target band5.0 – 6.5
Timing20 minutes
Worth⅓ of Writing
New strategy1 (the skeleton)
Part 1

What Task 1 actually is

You are given one table, graph, chart, or diagram and asked to describe it in at least 150 words. That's all. Not explain why it happened. Not say if it's good or bad. Not compare it to your own country unless the task tells you to. Describe what the numbers show.

The single most common beginner mistake

Writing an opinion. "I think internet access is important because…" scores zero for Task Achievement, no matter how good the English is. Task 1 has no opinions in it anywhere. If you catch yourself writing "I think" or "in my opinion," stop — that sentence belongs in Task 2, not here.

Quick check: Task 1 or Task 2?

Part 2 · The one strategy this lesson teaches

The four-part skeleton

Every Task 1 answer — table, line graph, bar chart, pie chart, it does not matter which — is built from the same four parts, in the same order. Learn this once and you can write about any data you're given.

1

Paraphrase the question

Rewrite the task sentence in your own words. Don't copy it — examiners notice, and copied words don't count toward your 150.

2

Overview

Two sentences stating the two or three biggest, most general patterns — no numbers, no small details. This is worth more marks than any other single part of the answer, and it's the part almost every beginner skips.

3

Body paragraph 1

Group roughly half the data logically (e.g. the two highest categories, or the first half of the time period) and support it with specific numbers.

4

Body paragraph 2

Cover the remaining data the same way. Together, Body 1 and 2 should mention every number the overview referred to in general terms.

Why the Overview matters most

A candidate who writes a perfect Overview and a messy set of numbers still scores higher than one who lists every number perfectly but never steps back to say what it all means. The Overview proves you understood the data — the body proves you can support it.

Part 3 · The one grammar point this lesson teaches

Past simple, not present perfect

Almost every Task 1 table or graph gives you a data period with a clear end point — "between 2005 and 2020," "in 2010 and 2022." Because that period is finished, describe it with the past simple, not the present perfect.

Turkish L1 trap

Turkish has no direct equivalent of the English present perfect, so learners either avoid it completely or overuse it in the wrong place — and Task 1 is exactly the wrong place, because the time period is closed, not open until now.

✕ "The number of visitors has increased between 2000 and 2020." ✓ "The number of visitors increased between 2000 and 2020."

Save the present perfect for the rare graph that says "up to the present" or "so far" with no fixed end date — you won't need it often at this level.

Turkish L1 trap — prepositions and articles with numbers

Two more errors this task produces constantly:

✕ "South Korea had highest rate, compared with Germany." (missing article) ✓ "South Korea had the highest rate, compared with Germany." ✕ "Brazil's figure was higher than the Kenya." (wrong preposition/article pattern) ✓ "Brazil's figure was higher than Kenya's." / "…compared with Kenya."

Fixed rule: the number of, the majority of, the highest/lowest always take "the." Comparisons use than (no preposition needed) or compared with/to — never "compared than" or "higher from."

Part 4 · Six words to actually learn today

The active set

Six words. Learn these properly before adding more — a longer list you half-remember is worth less than six you can use without thinking.

increaseverb / noun

"Access increased sharply between 2010 and 2022."

decrease / fallverb / noun

"Fiction borrowing fell over the period."

fluctuateverb

"Visitor numbers fluctuated before rising steadily."

Part 5 · Worked example — Table

No shape, only comparison

A table has no visual trend line, so instead of describing a "shape," you compare categories against each other and, if there are two years, each category against itself over time.

Task: The table below shows the percentage of households with high-speed internet access in four countries in 2010 and 2022.

Country20102022
South Korea78%98%
Germany60%95%
Brazil15%62%
Kenya3%22%
Paraphrase

The table illustrates the proportion of homes with fast internet connections in four different countries at two points in time, 2010 and 2022.

Overview

Overall, access to high-speed internet rose in every country over the period shown, although South Korea and Germany remained far ahead of Brazil and Kenya throughout. The most striking growth occurred in Brazil, while Kenya still had the lowest level of access by the end of the period.

Body 1

In 2010, South Korea had by far the highest rate of household internet access, at 78%, compared with 60% in Germany. Both countries saw further increases over the next twelve years, reaching 98% and 95% respectively in 2022, meaning that almost every home in these two nations was connected by the end of the period.

Body 2

Brazil and Kenya started from much lower figures. Only 15% of Brazilian households and just 3% of Kenyan households had high-speed internet in 2010. Although both countries recorded increases, the rise was considerably sharper in Brazil, whose figure jumped to 62% by 2022, than in Kenya, which reached only 22% over the same period.

  • reaching X and Y respectively — reports two numbers in one clause instead of two separate sentences. Saves words, sounds fluent.
  • compared with / than — the two comparison patterns from Part 3, both used correctly here.
  • Every verb is past simple (rose, had, reached, jumped) — the period 2010–2022 is closed.
  • The Overview names the pattern (everyone rose, two groups, Brazil's growth, Kenya's low end) before a single number appears.
Part 6 · Worked example — Line graph

Same skeleton, one new thing: the shape

A line graph does have a shape over time — so, on top of the same four parts, describe how the line moves: steady, sharp, gradual, a dip, a peak.

Task: The graph below shows the number of visitors (in thousands) to a national park between 2000 and 2020.

Paraphrase

The line graph illustrates how many people visited a national park over a twenty-year period, from 2000 to 2020.

Overview

Overall, the number of visitors to the park increased substantially over the two decades, despite a brief decline in the middle of the period. The most rapid growth took place in the final five years shown.

Body 1

In 2000, approximately 40,000 people visited the park. This figure rose to around 55,000 by 2005, before falling back slightly to 50,000 in 2010.

Body 2

From 2010 onwards, visitor numbers climbed steadily, almost doubling to reach 90,000 in 2015. This upward trend continued even more sharply over the next five years, and by 2020 the park was welcoming approximately 130,000 visitors annually — well over three times the figure recorded in 2000.

  • The Overview mentions the dip (2005–2010) and the acceleration (2015–2020) — the two features that make this graph interesting, stated with zero numbers.
  • climbed steadily vs. even more sharply — two different rates of change, named precisely instead of just "increased" twice.
  • "Well over three times the figure recorded in 2000" — a comparison back to the start point, a strong closing move for a body paragraph.
Part 7 · Your turn

Write one Overview — that's all

Not the whole answer. Just the Overview. Getting this one part right is worth more than a rushed full answer today.

Task: The table below shows the number of books borrowed from a public library in 2015 and 2023, by category.

Category20152023
Fiction4,2003,100
Non-fiction2,8004,600
Children's books5,0007,900

Look for two general patterns before you write a single number. Hint: one category moved differently from the other two.

Model overview

Overall, the total number of books borrowed increased between 2015 and 2023, driven mainly by strong growth in non-fiction and children's titles. Fiction was the only category to see a decline over the period.

For the teacher

How to run this

  1. This is Session 1 of a Task 1 sequence, not the whole topic. Task 1 has six visual types in total (line, bar, pie, table, process, map). Teaching all six in one sitting guarantees none of them stick — this lesson deliberately covers only table and line graph, because they share the same skeleton and the comparison learned here transfers directly.
  2. Suggested split: Parts 1–4 (skeleton + grammar + vocab) in one 40-minute session; Parts 5–7 (worked examples + practice) in a second session once the skeleton is genuinely memorised, not just seen once.
  3. Homework: one task only — write the full four-part answer for the library-books table above, 150 words, no time limit. Calibrated to finish in one sitting; do not assign a second table this week.
  4. Next session: bar chart and pie chart, reusing this exact skeleton — tell students explicitly that it's the same four parts again, to build confidence rather than presenting it as new content.
  5. Pair with: Cambridge IELTS 17–19, Writing Task 1 tables and line graphs, for authentic exposure once the skeleton is solid.
